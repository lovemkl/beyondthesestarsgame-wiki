Your first city on Kewa's back will teach you the game — the only question is whether it teaches gently or expensively. This guide plans the opening hours deliberately: where to land, what to build first, the supply loop that must be running before anything else, and the expansion timing that separates a confident start from a struggling one. It is written from launch-week play and assumes no prior city-builder depth; veterans can skim the placement rules and take the timing advice. Follow it once, and your second city is where the real game begins.

## Landing: Choosing Where the Story Starts

The game gives you the whale's back and asks where to begin, and the choice matters more than it looks. Beyond These Stars frames this moment as your founding decision, and the principles travel in order of importance. **Flat-ish ground first.** Kewa's anatomy offers plateaus and slopes, and slopes cost you — buildings and paths on steep curves consume space and walking time you will want back later. **Resources second.** Your first district needs wood and food within a short walk; a beautiful landing site with nothing to harvest is a famine with a view. **Room to grow third.** The best first district in Beyond These Stars has open land beyond its first ring, because the biggest beginner mistake is boxing yourself in with buildings you cannot move cheaply later.

One reassurance while you choose: Beyond These Stars is a forgiving teacher. Nothing here permanently ruins a save, and the whale's whole back is eventually yours. The placement discipline this guide teaches is about efficiency and momentum, not survival — the game respects your care, and this guide helps you spend it well.

## The First Hour: Survival Basics

The opening sequence, in the order that avoids the classic stalls:

**Housing before population arrives.** Pequeninos need beds from the first night; place the initial homes near your landing point, tightly clustered, with a path to the planned work sites. Clustered housing near clustered work is the game's first placement law — walking time is the invisible resource that decides whether your early loops hum or limp.

**Food: the simplest possible loop.** A forager or farm within reach of the housing, a food store or kitchen beside it, and a path connecting the three. Do not overbuild — one reliable loop beats two starving ones. The supply chain guide covers the general theory; at this stage, the rule is distance: every step from field to table should be a short walk.

**Wood: the same law applied.** A woodcutter near trees, a store near the woodcutter, paths between. Wood is your flexibility resource in the first hour — it becomes the buildings that fix problems, so a steady trickle beats feast-and-famine.

**Paths everywhere work happens.** Unpaved walking is slow walking, and slow walking is the tax every beginner pays without noticing. Connect everything with the cheapest path type as you place it, not as an afterthought; the minute of pathing now is the saved hour later.

By the end of the first hour you should have: a tight housing cluster, one food loop and one wood loop both running without your attention, and paths tying them together. That is a real city's seed, and everything later is this pattern at larger scale and deeper chains.

## The Second Hour: Stability and the First Schools

Once survival loops run themselves, Beyond These Stars opens its next layer — and the correct response is restraint. The tempting moves are expansion and pretty districts; the correct moves are stability investments. Education arrives here: your Pequeninos begin wanting more than food and beds, and schools are how the population grows into the workers the deeper chains demand. Place the first school inside the existing district — not in the new expansion you are dreaming about — so the citizens it serves can actually reach it. The buildings-and-people page covers the full needs ladder; at this stage the rule is simply: satisfy the need that exists, not the one you anticipate. A Beyond These Stars city that climbs its needs ladder in order is quietly compounding advantage every hour; one that skips rungs is quietly compounding debt.

Storage deserves the second hour's second thought. A central store between your production sites does more for stability than any new building type — it buffers the loops against timing hiccups and becomes the anchor the next district organizes around. On a curved map, central-between-things is a geometric claim, not a vibes claim: place the store at the walking-time midpoint of what it serves, and let the whale's shape tell you where that is.

## When to Expand — and When Absolutely Not To

The expansion timing rule, learned the expensive way by every new mayor: **expand when your loops are boring, not when they are fragile.** A new district is a set of problems — housing, food, work, paths — added to a city that must simultaneously keep running. If the existing loops still need your attention, a second district divides your focus and both suffer. If the first district runs while you watch — workers fed, stores filling, no bottleneck nagging — the city has earned its growth, and the expansion will feel like momentum instead of rescue.

The corollary: when you do expand, expand along the whale's grain. Kewa's back has natural corridors — flatter runs where districts chain along a path spine — and districts that follow them cost less than districts that fight across the curves. The whale is trying to help you; let it. This is the quiet genius of how Beyond These Stars teaches terrain: the map itself nudges beginners toward the shapes that work, and the punishment for ignoring it is walking time rather than ruin — a gentle curriculum, but a real one.

## The Three Beginner Mistakes, Named

Every struggling first city in Beyond These Stars is traceable to one of three causes. **Sprawl**: buildings scattered across the slopes, each individually sensible, collectively a city where every walk is long — the fix is ruthless clustering and honest paths. **Chain debt**: production buildings placed without their inputs nearby, so workers spend their day walking instead of working — the fix is building chains end-to-end, extractor beside processor beside store, before starting the next one. **Need blindness**: the city is fed and housed but unhappy, because the needs ladder climbed while the mayor wasn't looking — the fix is checking what Pequeninos are asking for before building what the mayor wants. Each mistake is one habit; each habit is this guide's placement laws wearing different hats.

## Your First Evening, Planned

Concretely: land with this guide open, spend the first hour on the survival loops exactly as sequenced, the second hour on stability and the first school, and stop there — an evening that ends with a stable, boring, running city is a perfect first session. The next session expands deliberately; the one after that meets the deeper chains and the journey's next chapter. Beyond These Stars paces itself kindly to mayors who pace themselves kindly, and the complete guide's promise holds here in miniature: build with the curves, cluster what walks, and the whaleback grows a city that feels like yours by the third night. See you at the second star.

A last word on the mindset that fits this game best, because it is the one thing no checklist supplies. Beyond These Stars is a builder of small correct decisions compounded — one loop placed well, one school sited honestly, one expansion timed patiently — and the mayors who thrive are the ones who enjoy making each small decision on its own merits rather than rushing past it toward some imagined finished city. The whale is not going anywhere fast, the stars are not going anywhere at all, and the Pequeninos are happiest when their mayor is unhurried. Treat the opening hours as the game's overture rather than its obstacle, and everything this guide has sequenced will feel less like instructions and more like the natural order of a world that was always going to be built this way.
