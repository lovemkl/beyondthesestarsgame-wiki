Beyond These Stars asks city builders one of the genre's most beautiful questions: what if the land itself was alive? Developed by Balancing Monkey Games and published by strategy specialists Hooded Horse, the game released at full 1.0 on October 16, 2026 — and it puts your civilization on the back of Kewa, a space whale swimming through the galaxy, with every farm, factory and school you build resting on that living, curved landscape. The short version for readers in a hurry: Beyond These Stars is a single-player city builder where your people — the Pequeninos — settle on a space whale's back and journey through the stars. You manage supply chains, citizen needs, education and expansion across a curved, breathing world. It launched complete on Steam and the Epic Games Store with a 20% launch discount, deliberately skipping its originally planned early access phase. It is the semi-sequel to the studio's planet-hopping Before We Leave, but you need no prior knowledge to start. If you build cities in games, this one deserves your evening.

## The Premise: A City on a Living World

The premise does more work than a setting usually does. In most city builders, terrain is static — hills to flatten, rivers to bridge, coastlines to exploit. In Beyond These Stars, the terrain is Kewa: a creature whose back is your entire buildable world, and whose journey through the galaxy is the campaign's spine. Buildings follow the whale's curves. Districts form along its anatomy — near the fin, along the spine, toward the tail. And the journey itself changes what the game asks of you: as Kewa swims, the stars outside shift, the story unfolds, and your people's search for purpose and answers drives what the city must become.

The Pequeninos, your citizens, carry the narrative. They are small, they are curious, and they have needs that evolve with the city — food and shelter first, then education, purpose and the deeper satisfactions a civilization discovers about itself. The game's tone sits in the warm corner of the genre: no disasters for sport, no punished failure states, but real logistical demands that reward planning. Think of it as a builder that respects your care rather than testing your reflexes.

## The Studio and the Publisher

Balancing Monkey Games is a New Zealand studio with a history this game grows directly out of. Their previous title, Before We Leave, put Pequeninos on planets after an age underground — a cozy, hex-based civilization rebuilder that earned a following for exactly the qualities Beyond These Stars extends: gentle tone, genuine logistics, and a world you wanted to take care of. The new game is a semi-sequel: same universe, same little people, radically different ground to build on.

The publisher matters too. Hooded Horse has become the strategy genre's most trusted name — a house whose logo on a box signals competence to strategy players, from grand strategy to colony sims. For a mid-sized city builder, that partnership meant launch-day visibility among exactly the audience most likely to love it, and it shows in the reception: launch coverage treated the game as an event, not an early access curiosity.

## The Launch That Skipped Early Access

The release story is genuinely unusual and worth understanding, because it shapes what you buy today. Beyond These Stars was originally planned for early access — a year of public iteration, feedback loops, roadmap promises. In March 2026, the studio announced it was skipping that phase entirely and shipping the game complete. And on October 16, 2026, that is what happened: a full 1.0, feature-complete, on Steam and the Epic Games Store, with a 20% launch discount on Steam.

Why does this matter to you as a player? Because "what you buy" questions that haunt early access buyers simply do not apply. There is no waiting for promised features, no roadmap roulette, no half-built systems wearing "we'll fix it later" badges. What shipped is the game — the complete campaign on Kewa's back, the systems the community is now documenting at speed, and a developer with a track record of supporting their games after launch. ## What Your First Hours Look Like

Without spoiling the journey's story beats, the opening arc teaches the game's grammar. You land on Kewa's back with a small band of Pequeninos. First needs are simple: food and wood, a place to sleep, the first dirt paths connecting them. The game introduces supply chains almost immediately — goods that must be extracted, moved, processed and delivered by workers who live within reach of their jobs. The whale's curvature matters from the start: distances are real, walking time is real, and a farm placed thoughtlessly far from its kitchen is a lesson you only need to learn once.

The pacing is the studio's signature: forgiving but never empty. New systems arrive as the city earns them — education when the basics are stable, deeper production chains when the first loop runs itself, the journey's next chapter when your people are ready to ask what lies beyond the next starfield. The beginner's first city guide on this wiki walks this arc step by step, with the placement rules and early priorities that save an evening of trial and error.
