Before We Leave players watching Beyond These Stars' launch are asking one question: is this the same game with a whale, or a different game with the same people? The honest answer is the interesting one — it is both, by design. This page compares the two directly: what carried over, what the whale genuinely changed, where each game is the better pick, and what the semi-sequel relationship means for the studio's trajectory. It concludes, because comparisons should.

## Same Universe, Same People, Different Ground

The lineage is explicit and warm. Before We Leave (2020) put the Pequeninos — small, gentle, post-catastrophe people — back onto planets after generations underground, and asked you to rebuild their civilization across hexagonal worlds with the same cozy logistical bones: supply chains, needs, a tone that respected care over crisis. Beyond These Stars inherits the universe, the little people, and the studio's entire design temperament. Players of the original will recognize the humor in the details, the pace of the systems, and the feeling of a game that wants you to succeed gently. The family resemblance is constant and deliberate.

The ground, however, is not inherited — it is the revolution. Before We Leave built on planets: hexes, flat-ish maps, the genre's familiar spatial grammar. Beyond These Stars builds on Kewa, a living space whale whose curved back replaces every assumption that grammar carried. The comparison below keeps returning to this because everything downstream flows from it: the placement puzzles, the district shapes, the journey-as-campaign — the whale is not Before We Leave's packaging difference; it is its mechanical heart transplant.

## What Carried Over

The inheritance list, for the original's fans deciding how much of their knowledge transfers. **The Pequeninos and their needs-ladder philosophy** — survival through comfort toward something like purpose, served by placement-smart social buildings. **The supply chain grammar** — extract, process, deliver, with walking time as the hidden cost; Before We Leave instincts about clustering chains transfer almost wholesale. **The tone** — forgiving, warm, anti-crisis-porn; a builder that respects your care. **The studio's update posture** — Balancing Monkey Games supported Before We Leave for years post-launch, which is a real signal for what Beyond These Stars' next year looks like. If you were good at the original, you are already good at this game's fundamentals — the differences are spatial and narrative, not systemic.

## What the Whale Changes

Three changes, in ascending order of consequence. **Shape**: the curved, anatomical buildable surface replaces hexes — placement decisions gain a dimension (walking over terrain, districts along anatomy) and lose one (no more tile-perfect grid plans). **Scale and boundary**: a whale's back is finite and knowable in a way planets were not; the game trades Before We Leave's multi-planet expansion sprawl for depth on one continuous, characterful surface — a smaller map that plays bigger because every meter has identity. **And the journey**: where Before We Leave's campaign hopped planets toward restoration, Beyond These Stars travels between stars toward purpose and answers — a campaign with a stronger narrative spine, tied to Kewa's voyage and the starfield's slow change. The whale converts the original's spatial sandbox into something closer to a journey with city-building inside it — the biggest design swing of the sequel, and the reason it is a semi-sequel rather than a patch.

## Which Game for Which Builder

The recommendation, by person. **The cozy-builder who missed both**: start with Beyond These Stars — it is the studio's current best expression, complete at 1.0, and plays standalone entirely; Before We Leave then becomes a back-catalog delight rather than homework. **The Before We Leave veteran**: buy the sequel for launch-week play, no hesitation — your skills transfer, the whale renews the spatial game you mastered, and the comparison this page sketches is the pleasure of watching a studio level up in front of you. **The player who specifically wants multi-planet scale**: the original remains the only place the Pequeninos hop worlds — the sequel's finite whaleback is a different fantasy, and Before We Leave scratches an itch its sequel deliberately does not. **The newcomer with a tight budget**: the launch-discounted sequel at its price floor beats the discounted original unless cozy-hex nostalgia specifically calls — both are good; the newer one is better supported from here.

One honest note on difficulty of entry: neither game gates the other. Beyond These Stars teaches itself from its first landing; Before We Leave taught itself too. The semi-sequel relationship is enrichment for those who know both, never a prerequisite for either.

## The Bottom Line

Before We Leave was a charming civilization-rebuilder that earned its following; Beyond These Stars is what its studio learned, aimed at a bigger swing — same people, same heart, radically more interesting ground to stand on. The comparison's verdict is unusually clean for this genre: play the sequel first, play the original whenever the mood for planets takes you, and be glad the studio that made the gentlest post-apocalypse in gaming decided its next world should be a whale. Few sequels keep so much of what worked while changing so much of what matters; this one does, and the whaleback is open to everyone.
